lumentoKEEP

Don't lose your memories. KEEP watches your NAS for silent file damage, the kind that no one notices until the photo won't open. It only reads. When a file has gone bad, it tells you which one and where a good copy may be.

The promise

It changes nothing you value.

KEEP only reads your files, and the Linux kernel stops it from writing to them. It never repairs, never copies over a file and never writes into a share. The only things it writes are its own index, its own pages and its own log lines.

That is a deliberate limit. A tool that fixes files can also break them. KEEP finds the problem and you decide what to do, in ADM, with your own hands.

What it does

Notice it early.
Know where to look.

Finds silent damage

A file can change on disk with no error and no warning. KEEP keeps a SHA-256 fingerprint of each file and checks it again later. If the bytes changed and nothing you did explains it, KEEP flags the file.

Tells an edit from damage

If you edit a photo, that is not damage. KEEP looks at the file in pieces to tell the two apart. It never stops to ask you.

Names the file

You get the file's name and what is wrong with it: damaged, unreadable or missing. You don't get a vague warning that something somewhere is off.

Points to a good copy

KEEP says where an intact copy may be, such as a snapshot or a backup. You restore it yourself in ADM.

Checks your safety net

Backups can fail without a word. KEEP also looks at your backup jobs, your snapshots, your disk scrubs and your RAID state, and tells you when one of them isn't doing its job.

Speaks through ADM

Notices go to ADM's own System log, where you already look. An admin page shows what KEEP has found, and you can acknowledge a finding there.

Reads encrypted shares

Encryption protects a drive that is carried away. It doesn't protect against rot. KEEP reads encrypted shares too, so they are not left out.

Light on the NAS

It runs at idle priority, pauses while the RAID syncs or scrubs, and works within a nightly limit on how much it reads.

Honest about its limits

Some file systems already checksum your data and some, like ext4, don't. KEEP says which you have, and says plainly when it can't promise something.

How it works

Index, compare,
tell you.

Your NAS may already catch some damage. KEEP adds the part it can't: a second, independent record of what each file should contain.

  1. Install lumento KEEP from App Central and set a password on its admin page.
  2. Let it index. Each night it reads your files and records a fingerprint for each. It throttles itself, so the NAS stays usable.
  3. It compares. On the next pass, a file whose contents changed with no matching edit is flagged. A file that is now unreadable or missing is flagged too.
  4. You hear about it. KEEP writes to ADM's System log and shows the finding on its admin page, with where a good copy may be.
  5. You restore. Use ADM to bring back the good copy. KEEP never does it for you.
