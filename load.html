lumentoLOAD

LOAD has one job: get every photo and video off your iPhone and onto your own NAS, byte for byte, without fighting SMB or AirDrop, and then tell you, with a number you can check, which ones are verified and on your NAS. Thousands at once, or each night while it charges.

FreeiPhone · iOS 18 and laterKeeps going when lockedNo cloud
Three choices

Load All. Load Album.
Pick where it goes.

  • Load All sends your whole library, every frame of every burst.
  • Load Album sends just one of your own albums.
  • Destination is the folder on your NAS: your default one, or one your NAS admin set up, such as a folder for each child. LOAD remembers your last choice.

Turn on "Back up new photos while charging" and LOAD sends what's new by itself while the phone charges, usually overnight.

The one promise

“1,204 of 1,204 assets verified and on your NAS.”

LOAD never says “uploaded” or “sync complete”. It says “verified”, and it means the bytes on your NAS were checked against the bytes that left your phone.

Then it's your call. LOAD never removes anything from your phone by itself. If you ask it to, it checks each photo against the NAS again first, asks you batch by batch, and tells you why it held any back.

Speed and stamina

27 GB in 37 minutes.
With the phone locked.

You can use LOAD two ways, and it's fast both ways.

Lock it and walk away

Tap Load, plug the phone in and lock it. Uploads run through iOS's own background upload service, so they keep going with the screen locked and even after the app has been closed. In our test, a locked phone loaded 26.9 GB, 1,228 photos and videos, in 37 minutes, with zero failures. Every file was hash-checked on the NAS.

With the app open

Faster still. LOAD adjusts how many files it sends at once to suit your phone's temperature and battery. With LOAD open, the same 27 GB loaded in under 10 minutes, and large videos went across at up to 78 MB a second.

Your results may vary. These are our numbers, on an iPhone 16, two Wi-Fi 6 routers and an Asustor NAS. We also ran LOAD on an iPhone XR, 12, 14 and 15 Pro, and they looked nearly as fast, though we didn't time them. The older the phone, the slower the run. Your speed depends on your Wi-Fi, your router, your NAS and your phone. When the phone is locked, iOS decides how much time LOAD gets, so a locked Load takes longer than one with the app open.

For the fastest run, keep LOAD open. If you lock the phone, plug it in.

Reliability

Built for the only copy of your photos.

Hashed at both ends

Every file gets a SHA-256 fingerprint on the phone and another on the NAS, from the bytes that actually arrived. If they don't match, the file is never stored.

Resumes where it stopped

Large files go in 8 MB chunks, each with its own hash. If the connection drops 95% of the way through a 4 GB video, LOAD carries on from the last confirmed chunk. It picks up the same way after the app is closed, iOS suspends it, or the Wi-Fi drops.

Checks for duplicates first

LOAD asks the NAS what it already has before sending any bytes, so photos already in the archive are skipped, even ones another device loaded. In our test, a second Load of 1,224 photos and videos settled in 9 seconds.

Never re-encoded

The files in your archive are the same bytes your camera wrote, so the date, place, camera and lens details inside each one come with it. Edited photos come across together with their originals.

A Live Photo stays whole

A Live Photo only counts as archived once both its still and its video are verified. RAW+JPEG pairs and edited photos work the same way: nothing counts until every part is verified.

Named by when you took it

On the NAS, each file is named by the moment it was taken, like 2026.10.19 20.42.21.000.HEIC, in a folder for its month, so your archive sorts itself.

Coming before release · Generic mode

Works with any NAS.
No install needed.

Generic mode is for you if you'd rather not install lumento STORE, or your NAS can't run it: for now STORE runs only on Asustor. It lets LOAD write straight to any NAS that can serve WebDAV over HTTPS, including Asustor, Synology and QNAP. On most of them that's a setting you turn on, not an app you install.

Generic mode is designed and being built. LOAD won't be released without it. It's described here as it's intended to work.

How it works

Why WebDAV and not SMB. WebDAV runs over HTTP, so LOAD can hand each upload to iOS's background upload service, the same one Verified mode uses. An SMB connection runs on raw sockets inside the app, and it stops as soon as iOS suspends the app. SMB isn't slower on a LAN. WebDAV was chosen because it's the one that keeps going when the phone is locked.

Writing a file. Each file is uploaded under a temporary .part name and then moved to its final name in one step. A half-finished upload never appears under a real filename.

PUT  _lumento/2026.09/.2026.09.14 18.03.52.120.HEIC.{device}.{id}.part
MOVE → _lumento/2026.09/2026.09.14 18.03.52.120.HEIC   (Overwrite: F)

Same layout as Verified mode. LOAD names and files each photo by the same rules STORE uses: {root}/_lumento/{YYYY.MM}/, named by when it was taken. Your archive looks the same whichever mode filled it.

Checked after it lands. LOAD fingerprints every file in small blocks as it sends it. Once the file is in place, it asks the NAS for its size and reads back a few blocks at random to compare. LOAD judges it once, when the file arrives; nothing re-checks it later.

Duplicate checking moves to the phone. LOAD keeps a local list of every file it's sent, with its hash, and a copy of that list on the NAS itself, so a reinstalled app still knows what it sent. If you load photos it already copied, it asks before sending them again.

Whole files only. WebDAV has no standard way to put chunks back together, so a dropped upload restarts that one file from the beginning.

Private by design

Your phone, your NAS.
Nothing in between.

LOAD finds lumento STORE on your home network, pairs with it, and sends your photos there and nowhere else. Pairing is started from the NAS with a six-digit code that never crosses the network, works only on your local network, and fixes the NAS's certificate so the phone only ever talks to that NAS. "Forget lumento STORE" on the phone removes the phone from the NAS too, when the NAS can be reached.

There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. See the privacy policy.
