lumentoLOAD

LOAD has one job: get the photos and videos on your iPhone safely onto your own NAS, and then tell you, with a number you can check, when it's safe to delete them from the phone.

iPhone · iOS 18 and laterFire-and-forget, at real scaleNo cloud
The one promise

“1,204 of 1,204 assets verified.
Safe to delete from iPhone.”

LOAD never says “uploaded” or “sync complete”. It says “verified”, and it means the bytes on your NAS were checked against the bytes that left your phone. Everything on this page exists to make that sentence true.

Speed and stamina

Very fast when it's open.
Keeps going overnight.

You can use LOAD two ways, and it's built for both.

With the app open

LOAD goes as fast as it can. It adjusts how many files it sends at once to suit your phone's temperature and battery. In our testing, large videos went across at tens of megabytes a second.

Plugged in at night

Start a Load in the evening, plug the phone in and lock it. Uploads run through iOS's own background upload service, so they keep going with the screen locked and even after the app has been closed. In our latest overnight test, a phone that was plugged in and locked loaded about 27 GB, 1,227 files, with zero failures by morning.

Locked, iOS sets the pace, and it's a slow one. On our test hardware a locked phone ran at roughly a twelfth of the speed it reaches with LOAD open. It still finishes and nothing is lost. It just takes longer.

We don't recommend leaving it unplugged and locked. Keep the app open for the fastest run, or plug the phone in and let it work overnight.

Reliability

Built for the only copy of your photos.

Hashed at both ends

Every file gets a SHA-256 fingerprint on the phone and another on the NAS, from the bytes that actually arrived. If they don't match, the file is never stored.

Resumes where it stopped

Large files go in 8 MB chunks, each with its own hash. If the connection drops 95% of the way through a 4 GB video, LOAD carries on from the last confirmed chunk instead of starting again.

Checks for duplicates first

LOAD asks the NAS what it already has before sending any bytes. Photos that are already in the archive are skipped, even ones another device loaded.

Never re-encoded

The files in your archive are the same bytes your camera wrote. Edited photos come across together with their originals.

A Live Photo stays whole

A Live Photo is a still image plus a short video. It only counts as safe once both parts are verified. RAW+JPEG pairs work the same way.

Survives interruptions

A Load picks up where it left off after the app is closed, iOS suspends it, or the Wi-Fi drops. Failed uploads are retried after 5, 15, 45, 135 and then 300 seconds.

Won't fill your phone

LOAD pauses before it runs your phone out of storage. If a Load won't fit, it warns you before starting.

Deletes only when you say so

LOAD reports what is safe to delete. It only deletes when you ask it to, one batch at a time, and you confirm each batch.

Hidden stays hidden

If you hid a photo on your iPhone, LOAD never sees it, never reads it and never loads it.

Coming later · Generic mode

Works with any NAS.
No install needed.

lumento STORE gives you a complete, verified archive, but for now it only runs on Asustor. Generic mode will let LOAD write straight to any NAS that can serve WebDAV over HTTPS, including Asustor, Synology and QNAP. On most of them that's a setting you turn on, not an app you install.

Generic mode is designed but not built yet. It's described here as it's intended to work.

How it works

Why WebDAV and not SMB. WebDAV runs over HTTP, so LOAD can hand each upload to iOS's background upload service, the same one Verified mode uses. An SMB connection runs on raw sockets inside the app, and it stops as soon as iOS suspends the app. SMB isn't slower on a LAN. WebDAV was chosen because it's the one that keeps going when the phone is locked.

Writing a file. Each file is uploaded under a temporary .part name and then moved to its final name in one step. A half-finished upload never appears under a real filename.

PUT  _lumento/2026.09/.IMG_4821.HEIC.{device}.{id}.part
MOVE → _lumento/2026.09/IMG_4821.HEIC   (Overwrite: F)

Same layout as Verified mode. Folders, filenames and dates are identical: {root}/_lumento/{YYYY.MM}/. Your archive looks the same whichever mode filled it.

Duplicate checking moves to the phone. LOAD keeps a local list of every file it has sent, with its hash. It also keeps a manifest on the NAS itself.

Whole files only. WebDAV has no standard way to put chunks back together, so a dropped upload restarts that one file from the beginning.

Generic mode tells you what it can't check. It will say that files arrived, but not that they arrived byte for byte, because nothing on the NAS can check that. If you install lumento STORE later, it checks everything Generic mode already sent. You don't upload anything again.

Private by design

Your phone, your NAS.
Nothing in between.

LOAD finds lumento STORE on your home network, pairs with it, and sends your photos there and nowhere else. Pairing is started from the NAS, works only on your local network, and fixes the NAS's certificate so the phone only ever talks to that NAS.

There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. See the privacy policy.
