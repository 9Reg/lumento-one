lumentoONE questions

How playback, formats, ARM and Intel models, your library, signing in, card import and projects work, in plain words.

Playing and converting

Why some videos start at once.

ONE picks the cheapest way to show each video in the browser you're using. That's why one clip starts straight away and another says "Preparing".

Why do some videos play at once and others say "Preparing"?

ONE picks one of four ways, per video and per browser:

  1. Plays as it is. Your browser can play the file, so ONE just hands it over. Nothing is converted.
  2. Repackaged. Your browser can play the picture and sound but not the file's wrapper. ONE re-wraps it without re-encoding anything, which takes seconds.
  3. Converted. Your browser can't play the video, so ONE converts it to H.264 at no more than 1080p, without the camcorder steps below.
  4. Camcorder pipeline. Old camcorder and disc footage (AVCHD, MiniDV, DVD, anything interlaced) is deinterlaced, its picture shape is corrected and its sound is levelled. You can't skip ahead in it until the conversion finishes.

"Preparing" means the video is being repackaged or converted.

What happens the first time, and the next?

The first watch converts the video and ONE keeps the result in its cache. Later watches play from the cache and start at once. The cache holds 50 GB and keeps a video for 30 days since it was last watched, by default. A curator can change both. The cache is ONE's own, never inside your shares.

Why does ONE convert one video at a time?

Converting is heavy work for a NAS, and ONE does it quietly so browsing stays quick. By default it converts one video at once, and others wait their turn. A curator can raise that, up to four, under "Conversions at once" in Settings.

Sound and colour

Levelled sound, HDR and log.

A few things ONE does to a clip while it converts it, and a few it deliberately leaves alone.

Why is camcorder sound levelled but phone sound isn't?

Only the camcorder pipeline evens out loudness. Old recordings can be very quiet, so ONE brings them to a sensible volume. Today's phones record at a sensible level, so their sound stays exactly as recorded. Where the camcorder pipeline meets 5.1 surround sound, ONE mixes it down to stereo.

What does ONE do with HDR video and 60 frames a second?

HLG video, which is what an iPhone records, plays as it is. HDR10 video is converted to look right on an ordinary screen, so it isn't washed out. A converted video plays at half the frame rate when the original is above 30 (59.94 becomes 29.97, 50 becomes 25), which makes it convert sooner.

My camera's log footage looks grey. How do I fix it?

A camera's log mode records a flat, grey picture meant for grading. ONE shows it in normal colours. Sony cameras are recognised from the clip itself, so it just works. For other makers, a curator opens the clip in the modern viewer and picks its colour profile: Automatic, Normal, or one of the log profiles. The choice is per clip and ONE keeps it in its own database, so the video file isn't changed.

ARM and Intel models

Why a Drivestor is slower to convert.

What your NAS's processor can do decides how long a first watch takes. Photos, and videos your browser plays as they are, aren't affected on any model.

How slow are ARM models (Drivestor series)?

These models have no video hardware lumento can use, so a video that has to be converted is converted by the processor alone, and slowly. ONE uses a faster, less compact setting on them, so converted files in the cache are about three times larger. The first time you play such a video, expect to wait about two to four times its length before it starts: a 10-minute camcorder clip can take 20 to 40 minutes, and a 4K phone video much longer. After that first time it plays at once.

What does the graphics chip on Intel models do?

On models with an Intel graphics chip, ONE converts video on it, which is much faster than the processor alone. Each time it starts, ONE tests the chip. If the test fails, it converts on the processor for that run. Models whose processor has no Intel graphics chip, and AMD graphics, convert on the processor. On Intel models, a curator can turn the graphics chip off with the "Use the GPU" switch in Settings.

Library and folders

Your files, and who sees them.

ONE reads your folders where they are. It doesn't keep a second copy of your library.

Does ONE change my files?

No. ONE only reads your originals. A photo you rotate is remembered in ONE's own gallery database, and a clip's colour profile in its projects database. Its thumbnails and cache live outside your shares. The files themselves stay exactly as they were.

Which folders can ONE show?

Any folder inside any share, up to 100, not only the /Photos share the stock gallery needs. A curator picks them in Settings. The limits are on the specifications page. By default ONE skips ADM's recycle bin (#Recycle), files and folders whose names start with a dot, Thumbs.db, and any folder named private, in any capitals. A curator can review that list.

Who sees what?

People sign in with their NAS accounts, and each person sees only the folders ADM lets them read. Viewers can browse. Curators, which means members of ADM's administrators group, can also manage library folders and settings.

Formats and browsers

Old TVs, new browsers, Canon RAW.

The full list of formats is on the ONE page.

Will it work on my old TV's browser?

Yes. ONE has two front ends. The legacy one is for older browsers, such as 2013 Samsung smart TVs: plain pages whose controls are ordinary links. Browsing and viewing work there, even with JavaScript turned off. Current Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge, and a Fire TV Stick, get the modern front end automatically, and new features are built there.

What about Canon .cr3 files?

They show up in your library as named tiles that say they can't be displayed yet. They'll start displaying, with no re-indexing, once a CR3 decoder is added.

Signing in on a phone

Why your phone says "not private".

When you scan the TV's sign-in code, your phone moves from http://…:20904 to https://…:20907 before it asks for your password. That's a security measure. Over plain http, anyone on the same Wi-Fi could read your password as it crosses the network. Over https it's encrypted.

The warning is expected

Your phone will then say the connection isn't private. It isn't a fault. ONE encrypts the connection with a certificate it makes for your NAS itself, and your phone has no way to check who made it, so it asks you. If this is your own NAS at home, continue:

  • iPhone (Safari): tap Show Details, then visit this website, then Visit Website.
  • Android (Chrome): tap Advanced, then Proceed to … (unsafe).

Your phone remembers the choice. You'll see the warning again only when ONE has to make a new certificate: about every two years, or when the NAS gets a new network address.

If the page stays blank

Close it and scan the TV's code again. Safari sometimes remembers your choice but doesn't load the page that first time.

To get rid of the warning for good

Give the NAS a proper certificate in ADM, for example from Let's Encrypt, under ADM's certificate settings. ONE notices it within a minute and uses it instead of its own. Such a certificate names a domain (say nas.example.com), not an IP address, so open ONE on the TV by that name too. The phone follows the address the TV used.

Card import

Camera cards, step by step.

How a card gets from your reader onto the NAS is on the ONE page.

What does card import need?

lumento STORE installed and paired with ONE, and a current browser. Before it shows the import form, ONE tests what your browser can do, and if the test fails you get a plain explanation instead of a form that half works. Without STORE, ONE still works as a complete gallery. By default only curators can import, and a curator can let any signed-in person do it.

What happens to a Sony clip's extra files?

A Sony camera writes two small files beside each clip: its settings for that clip (an .XML) and a preview picture. STORE files both beside the clip under its new name, untouched, and ONE shows the clip once, never its preview as a photo of its own.

Projects

Picking photos for print.

A project is a list of photos you mark, such as a year's picks for a calendar.

What's in a project's zip?

The photos, ready to upload to a print shop. Videos stay on the project's list but aren't in the zip, because one camcorder clip can outweigh a hundred photos. The zip is limited to 250 MB by default, and a curator can set that anywhere from 10 MB to 2 GB. A list.txt inside names every file in the zip and where it came from, and lists any video or missing photo that was left out.

How many photos can a project hold?

Up to 250 marked photos and videos together.
